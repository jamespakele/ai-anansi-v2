## Concepts

- `#smart-brevity` — "a system and strategy for thinking more sharply, communicating more crisply and saving yourself and others time. It guides you into saying a lot more with a lot less"
- `#audience-first` — "the very first—and two most important—words of our company's manifesto... If you think about serving your audience first—colleagues, students, parishioners, friends—and not your own ego, you will naturally cut down on waste"
- `#lede` — "your opening sentence should be the most memorable—tell me something I don't know, would want to know, should know. Make this sentence as direct, short and sharp as possible"
- `#axiom` — bolded signpost headings (Why it matters, The big picture, The bottom line, etc.) that guide skimmers to the key point: "Axioms are like street signs: They tell you where you are and where you are going"
- `#go-deeper` — "an exit line from a Smart Brevity item that makes the reader feel fulfilled and allows you to point to context without a glob of words that's going to lose the audience"
- `#continuous-partial-attention` — Linda Stone's term for readers who are "constantly thinking about the next alert, text or email" even while looking at your words
- `#brevity-is-confidence` — "Brevity is confidence. Length is fear." — core mantra posted in the Axios newsroom
- `#word-count` — the explicit Smart Brevity practice of stating word count and estimated read time at the top of any communication
