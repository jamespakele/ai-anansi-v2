# Smart Brevity — Workplace memo / general work comms

> Layered on top of `general.md`. Apply both.
>
> Source: *Smart Brevity*, Ch. 14

---

## When this is the right format

- Internal communication aligning a team or org on a value, strategy, decision, or update
- One-to-many, asynchronous, expected to be skimmed
- Will live in a doc, Slack post, intranet, or wiki — not just an inbox

---

## Structure

```
# <Memo title — ≤6 words, action-shaped>

*<N> words, <M> minutes*

<First sentence — the news, ask, or decision. One sentence.>

**Why it matters:** <1–2 sentences of context for THIS team>

## What's changing

- <bullet — bold the key term>
- <bullet>
- <bullet>

## What's next

- <bullet — actions, owners, dates>
- <bullet>

## Go deeper

<Optional links / references for those who want detail>
```

---

## Workplace-memo specific rules

### Title
- ≤6 words. Action-shaped when possible: "New remote work plan," "Q3 priorities locked," "Sprint recap: 7 templates."
- Avoid "Update on..." or "Following up on..." — passive and weak.

### Opening sentence
- Lead with what's NEW. Not context, not background.
- One sentence.

### Section headings (axiom-style)
- Use bolded section headers like `## What's changing` / `## Why it matters` / `## What's next` / `## By the numbers`.
- Each section starts with the axiom's role, then 1–2 sentences or bullets.

### Bullets are mandatory for any list
- 3+ items → bullets, not prose.
- Bold the lead term in each bullet.
- One thought per bullet.

---

## Cadence and modeling (Ch. 14)

- **Weekly cadence works best.** Sunday or early Monday.
- **Morning send** maximizes open rates.
- **Leadership models it.** If leaders don't follow Smart Brevity, no one else will.
- Different teams can have their own newsletters with the same skeleton — leaders see what's happening across the org without one-on-ones.

Examples of team-newsletter naming from the book:
- "Lights On" (revenue)
- "Cranes" (local team)
- "Click Clack" (web traffic)
- "The Funnel" (growth)
- "The TopLine" (sales)
- "5 Big Things" (org-wide)

---

## Length targets

- Most memos: < 500 words.
- If genuinely 1,000+ words needed, structure as Part 1 / Part 2 with a TL;DR up front.
- A long memo with no TL;DR fails the audience-first test.

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Tone too formal / corporate | Write like you'd say it in a 1:1 |
| No "why it matters" | Add the bolded axiom + 1–2 sentences |
| Random one-off email | Establish a cadence; memos are habits |
| Wall of text | Bullets every 3+ points; visual breaks |
| Buried decision | First sentence = decision/ask |
| No clear owner / next step | "Who does what by when" goes in `## What's next` |
