# Smart Brevity — Email rules

> Layered on top of `general.md`. Apply both.
>
> Source: *Smart Brevity*, Ch. 15

---

## When this is the right format

- One specific decision, ask, update, or piece of news directed at one or more named recipients
- Asynchronous; the reader will see it in an inbox surrounded by other claims on attention
- 70% of employees want shorter messages; only 50% read leader emails. Plan for skim.

---

## Structure

```
Subject: <6-word tease — strong verb, concrete>

<First sentence — the news or ask. One sentence.>

**Why it matters:** <1–2 sentences of context for THIS reader>

- <bullet of supporting data, named with bold key terms>
- <bullet>
- <bullet>

<Optional: visual / link — the "go deeper">

— <signature>
```

---

## Subject line rules

- **6 words maximum.** Hard limit.
- Tells the recipient **why open NOW**.
- Strong verb. No "Re:" buildup, no "Following up," no "Quick question."
- If urgent, the urgency is in the words, not in marking it !!!.

**Bad → good:**
- "Following up on our discussion regarding the Q3 roadmap" → "Q3 roadmap: decision needed Friday"
- "Quick question" → "Need your sign-off on contract"
- "Just checking in" → "Status update on Acme deal"

---

## Opening sentence rules

- Lead with the **news or ask**. No preamble.
- One sentence.
- The recipient should know after this sentence whether to keep reading.

**Bad opening:**
> Hi James, I hope you're doing well. I wanted to reach out following our conversation last Tuesday about the proposal we discussed, and I was hoping to get some thoughts...

**Good opening:**
> Need your sign-off on the Acme contract by Friday — terms are below.

---

## Body rules

- **Why it matters:** is bold. 1–2 sentences. Why does this recipient need to know NOW?
- Bullets for any list of supporting data, options, dependencies.
- Bold key names, dates, dollar figures.
- Visuals welcome if they speed comprehension. Don't decorate.

---

## What to cut from typical emails

- "I hope you're doing well."
- "I just wanted to..."
- "Sorry to bother you..."
- "Following up on..." (lead with the new news instead)
- "As discussed..."
- Apology paragraphs. Apologize once or not at all.
- Recap of the entire backstory the recipient already knows.

---

## Length target

- One important point per email. If you have three, send three (or escalate to a call).
- Total under newsletter length — usually < 200 words.
- A 4-sentence email beats a 14-sentence email almost always.

---

## Common mistakes (and the fix)

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Lame subject line ("Quick question") | Specific, urgent, ≤6 words |
| Burying the lede | First sentence = the ask/news |
| Too much context up front | Move context into "Why it matters" |
| Dense paragraphs | Bullets for any 3+ points |
| Saying it twice | Pick the better sentence; cut the other |
| Throat-clearing in opening | Delete entirely; start with the news |
| Trailing "let me know if any questions" | Often unnecessary; trust the reader |
