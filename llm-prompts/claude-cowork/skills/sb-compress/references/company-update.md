# Smart Brevity — Company-wide update / leader-to-org rules

> Layered on top of `general.md` and `newsletter.md`. Apply all three.
>
> Source: *Smart Brevity*, Ch. 21

---

## When this is the right format

- CEO / leader to entire organization (or business unit)
- Weekly or bi-weekly cadence
- Aligns on values, strategy, culture, decisions; reduces silos and gossip
- Critical for distributed / hybrid / WFH teams

---

## Structure

This is a **newsletter** (see `newsletter.md`) authored by a leader. All newsletter rules apply, plus:

```
<Newsletter name — e.g., "5 Big Things">

*<word count>, <minutes> minutes*

## 1 big thing: <The most important alignment-shaping item this week>

<One sentence — the news / decision / signal>

**Why it matters:** <1–2 sentences — for the org, not just the leader>

- <bullet — bold the key term>
- <bullet>

---

## 2. <Second item — strategic / operational>
…

## 3. <Third item — milestone / win>
…

(5–7 items typical)

---

## 1 fun thing
<What the leader is reading, listening to, watching, or doing personally>
```

---

## Leader-specific rules (Jim's Tips, Ch. 21)

### 1. Mission matters
- Pull at least one item back to the **soul / purpose** of the org each week.
- "Why it matters" is the perfect device — context to mission.
- Repetition required. If you said it once, say it again next week.

### 2. Tell a story
- Over a year, the newsletters should tell **one cohesive story** about what you and the org were doing, thinking, accomplishing.
- Not 52 disconnected updates — a continuous narrative.

### 3. Don't be a fraud
- Write with **candor and authenticity.**
- People sniff out lawyered lines and corporate nonsense in a second.
- If legal/PR has rewritten it, the trust is gone.

### 4. Don't quit
- Connect at least weekly. **Resist the urge to skip.**
- Skip a week and the habit breaks for the whole org.
- Even when the week was hard. *Especially* when the week was hard.

### 5. Be humble
- Show gratitude.
- Admit mistakes.
- Poke fun at yourself.
- A leader who can't laugh at themselves can't lead.

### 6. Encourage copycats
- Magic happens when other execs / managers start doing the same thing for their teams.
- Examples in the book: each Axios exec runs their own team newsletter (Lights On, Cranes, Click Clack, The Funnel, The TopLine).
- This eliminates canned updates in 1:1s and exec meetings — everyone already knows what everyone's doing.

---

## Cascade pattern

```
CEO weekly newsletter ("5 Big Things")
    ↓
Each exec runs their own ("Lights On" for revenue, "The Funnel" for growth, etc.)
    ↓
Each manager runs their team's
    ↓
Everyone aligns; no one says "I didn't know"
```

## Length targets

Same as `newsletter.md`:

| Element | Limit |
|---|---|
| Items | 5–7 |
| Each item | ≤200 words |
| Whole newsletter | <1,000 words |
| "1 fun thing" | Genuinely human; can be brief |

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Inconsistent cadence | Same day, same time, every week. No exceptions. |
| Corporate / lawyered tone | Write the way you'd speak in a 1:1 |
| Hiding bad news | Address it honestly; people respect candor |
| No mission tie-in | At least one item per week pulls back to purpose |
| Items too long | Cap each at 200 words; link out for detail |
| Skipping "1 fun thing" | The human moment is what gets read, often forwarded |
| No cascade | Encourage execs to do their own; alignment compounds |
