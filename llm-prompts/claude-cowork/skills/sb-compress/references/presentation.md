# Smart Brevity — Presentation / slide deck rules

> Layered on top of `general.md`. Apply both.
>
> Source: *Smart Brevity*, Ch. 18 + Ch. 20

---

## When this is the right format

- Visual-verbal communication with slides accompanying spoken delivery
- 5–12 slides total (the McKinsey rule: for every 20 you want, use 2)
- Audience absorbs each slide in **3 seconds max**

---

## The two principles

1. **Simplify to exaggerate.** Fewer slides, fewer words, fewer visuals = sharper message. Cut anything that distracts from the essential point.
2. **One message per slide.** If a slide has more than one idea, split it.

---

## Pre-deck planning

Before opening the deck:

1. **Write down the precise outcome you want** in one sentence.
2. List the **3–5 supporting points** you must make to get there.
3. Sequence them like an argument before a jury.
4. Apply Smart Brevity to the ask itself — sharpen it to ~6 words.

If you can't do this exercise, the deck isn't ready to draft.

---

## Per-slide rules

### Slide construction

- **One message per slide.** Period.
- **≤20 words on a slide.** If you're past 20, try again.
- **One font.** One visual style. No mixed treatment.
- **Pictures > text.** Adding an arresting image lifts recall from 10% (audio only) to 65% (audio + image).
- **Never just read your slides.** If the audience can read it faster than you can speak it, you're decoration.

### The billboard test

Imagine someone driving past at 65 mph. Would they get this slide?

If no — too much text, too many ideas, too small a font. Cut.

### Headline-first

Each slide's headline IS its takeaway. Not a topic ("Q3 Results") but a takeaway ("Q3 revenue up 18% on enterprise wins").

The headline does the work. The visual reinforces it.

---

## Deck-level rules

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Total slides | 5–12; dozen max |
| Slide headline | ≤6 words OR a takeaway sentence |
| Slide body text | ≤20 words |
| Words per slide (incl. headline) | Aim under 25 |
| Cognitive load per slide | One idea |
| Final slide | The ask, distilled to fewest possible words |

---

## The "Always be closing" slide

The **final slide is the ask.** Fill in this blank:

> "I called this meeting and built this deck so I can get _____ or teach you _____."

Whatever fills the blank IS the final slide, distilled to the fewest possible words. If you can't write it in one sentence, you don't know what you're asking for.

## Visual hierarchy (Ch. 20)

Establish hierarchy through:

- **Scale** — bigger means more important
- **Color** — high contrast pulls the eye
- **Contrast** — bold against muted, dark against light
- **Whitespace** — what's NOT there guides the eye to what IS

Test by asking:
- Does the concept make sense to fresh eyes?
- Are all elements legible from the back row?
- Does layout express content, or fight it?
- Judge from the audience's perspective, not yours.

---

## What this skill produces

When asked to rewrite a presentation, output a **slide-by-slide outline** in Smart Brevity form — not flowing prose. Format:

```
# Deck: <topic title — ≤6 words>

**Outcome:** <one sentence — what you want the audience to do/believe after>

## Slide 1 — Tease (the hook)
**Headline:** <≤6 words>
- <≤20 words of body, if any>
- <visual cue: what image/graphic accompanies>

## Slide 2 — <Point 1 takeaway, not topic>
**Headline:** <takeaway, sentence-length OK if it earns it>
- <≤20 words body>

## Slide 3 — <Point 2 takeaway>
…

(5–12 slides total)

## Final slide — The Ask
**Headline:** <the ask, fewest possible words>
- <one line of context if needed>
```

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Death by PowerPoint (text-heavy slides) | ≤20 words per slide; pictures > text |
| Reading slides aloud | Slide is reinforcement; you are the message |
| 30-slide deck | Cut to 12. McKinsey rule: 2 for every 20 you want. |
| Generic stock images | Image must reinforce the specific takeaway |
| Topic headlines ("Q3 Results") | Takeaway headlines ("Q3 revenue +18%") |
| No clear ask | Final slide = the ask, distilled |
| Mixed fonts / styles | One font, one style, throughout |
| Auto-play transitions, sound effects | Cut. Distractions kill focus. |
