# Smart Brevity — Speech / talk rules

> Layered on top of `general.md`. Apply both.
>
> Source: *Smart Brevity*, Ch. 17

---

## When this is the right format

- Spoken remarks to a live audience on one specific topic
- 5–18 minutes typical; TED Talk standard caps at 18
- The audience will remember **one** point. Plan for that.

---

## Why short works

- Gettysburg Address: 272 words.
- JFK inaugural: under 15 minutes.
- TED Talks: 18 minutes max — and audiences remember them.
- Length doesn't equal weight. Sharpness does.

---

## Structure (Nancy Duarte model + Smart Brevity)

```
1. Open with a hook
   — A real-life story, a startling fact, or a question
   — Short: 30–60 seconds

2. Describe the status quo
   — How the world / topic exists today
   — Set the baseline the audience already knows

3. Contrast with your idea
   — The gap between what is and what could be
   — Move back and forth between current state and the vision

4. The Big Thought (state it explicitly)
   — "The one thing I want you to remember today is…"
   — Then the Big Thought, verbatim

5. Why it matters
   — One sentence of context
   — "This matters to you because…"

6. Numbered support points (3–5)
   — "Three reasons…" announced up top
   — Numbering helps note-taking and signals command

7. Call to action
   — One specific ask of the audience

8. Reinforce the Big Thought
   — "Remember, if there's one thing you take away…"
   — Verbatim repeat of the Big Thought

9. Close on gratitude
   — Brief, warm, real
```

---

## The Big Thought rule

- **Write it down word-for-word before drafting the speech.**
- **15 words maximum** (Harvard Business Review).
- One-syllable words win. Polish until every syllable earns its place.
- More provocative the better — would audience members rush to text it to a friend?

**Examples of strong Big Thoughts:**

- "Lying is a cooperative act." (Pamela Meyer, TED)
- "Brevity is confidence. Length is fear." (the book itself)
- "The only people for me are the mad ones." (paraphrased Kerouac, but the shape works)

---

## Hook rules

- **Real-life anecdote that ends with a chuckle**, OR
- **Startling fact**, OR
- **Provocative question**
- Length: like running into a neighbor — long enough to engage, short enough they don't wish you'd left
- Set up time/place → describe situation → tell what happened → STOP

**Pamela Meyer's hook (book example):**
> "I don't want to alarm anybody in this room, but it's just come to my attention that the person to your right is a liar."

---

## Delivery rules (for the speaker)

- **No slides.** No notes. No teleprompter.
- **Practice looking 5–6 specific people in the eyes.**
- **Speak like a human**, not a Broadway character.
- **Don't read from anything.** Memorize the Big Thought verbatim; everything else is scaffolding around it.

---

## Length targets

| Element | Limit |
|---|---|
| Total length | ≤18 minutes (TED standard) |
| Hook / opening | 30–60 seconds |
| Big Thought | ≤15 words |
| Why it matters | 1 sentence |
| Support points | 3–5, numbered |
| Big Thought repeats | Twice — once at start, once at end |

---

## What to write in the rewrite

When this skill rewrites a speech, it should produce a **speech outline** in Smart Brevity form — not flowing prose. Format:

```
# Speech: <topic title — ≤6 words>

*<spoken-word count>, ~<minutes> minutes*

## Hook (30–60 sec)
<Story / fact / question — written as the speaker should say it, but tight>

## The Big Thought (memorize verbatim)
"<≤15-word Big Thought>"

## Why it matters
<One sentence of context — speak this verbatim>

## Three / four / five points

1. **<Point 1 headline>** — <1–2 sentences of how to frame it>
2. **<Point 2 headline>** — <1–2 sentences>
3. **<Point 3 headline>** — <1–2 sentences>

## Call to action
<The one ask of the audience>

## Close
"Remember, if there's one thing you take away today — <Big Thought verbatim>."
```

---

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| No clear Big Thought | Write it word-for-word before drafting anything else |
| Multiple "main points" | Pick ONE. Support points orbit it; they don't compete. |
| Reading from notes | Memorize the Big Thought; everything else is improv on a frame |
| "But all kidding aside…" | If you're saying this, you've already lost them — start stronger |
| Generic, slow opening | Hook fast: story + chuckle, fact, or question |
| No numbered structure | Announce "three points" / "five reasons" — gives the audience scaffold |
| Too long | If >18 min, you're cutting an audience by ~half each extra 5 min |
